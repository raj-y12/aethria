# GTM And Moat V2

## The Wedge

Do not start by asking funds to pipe their entire proprietary pipeline into Aethria.

Start with a lower-trust, high-clarity wedge:

> "Give Aethria 20 historical deals: 5 you invested in, 5 you passed on and were right, 5 you passed on and regretted, 5 still ambiguous. We will show whether the system can reconstruct your judgment pattern and expose where your process breaks."

This creates a direct route into the customer's real pain: misses, false positives, stale maybes, lost context.

## Initial Customer

Best first customers:

- Emerging managers raising or deploying Fund I/II.
- Solo GPs with high deal volume and low institutional process.
- Micro-funds with analyst/platform help but weak IC tooling.
- Accelerators, scouts, and venture studios that evaluate many early companies.

Avoid starting with:

- Large established multi-stage firms with entrenched CRMs and security review.
- Funds that treat process software as a threat to partner autonomy.
- Firms unwilling to share even historical pipeline data.

## Why They Buy

The buyer does not buy "better AI analysis."

They buy:

- A cleaner Monday partner meeting.
- Fewer deals stuck in "interesting, follow up later."
- A record of why the firm passed or monitored.
- A way to train new analysts on the fund's judgment.
- IC packs that make risk, dissent, and missing evidence explicit.
- A system that looks institutional to LPs and future hires.

## Trust Path

### Phase 1 - Historical Postmortem

Use past deals, public sources, and the firm's own memory.

Goal: show Aethria can surface the actual reasons behind decisions and misses.

### Phase 2 - Shadow Mode

Aethria evaluates live deals without replacing the CRM or partner meeting.

Goal: earn trust by matching or usefully challenging partner intuition.

### Phase 3 - IC Pack Wedge

The firm uses Aethria for selected memos and risk registers.

Goal: become useful in the moment before partner discussion.

### Phase 4 - Decision Ledger

The firm records pass/monitor/invest rulings, rationale, and risk acknowledgements in Aethria.

Goal: make Aethria the memory of investment judgment.

### Phase 5 - Full Queue

Aethria becomes the weekly queue for unresolved opportunities.

Goal: own the operating cadence.

## Distribution Strategy

1. Warm VC network wedge through founder friends, scouts, angel syndicates, accelerator mentors, and emerging-manager communities.
2. Public teardown content: "Why funds miss obvious winners," "The Monday meeting operating system," "How to run a pass postmortem."
3. Design-partner program with 3-5 funds, explicitly not priced like enterprise SaaS.
4. Build trust via local/private data handling options and clear deletion/export controls.
5. Create artifacts that GPs already share: IC packs, postmortems, partner meeting queues.

## Pricing Hypothesis

Early pilots:

- Free or low-cost design partner if they commit weekly usage and feedback.
- Paid pilot for funds with live usage, likely $500-$2,000/month depending on volume and support.

Longer term:

- Fund-level platform fee, not seat-only pricing.
- Pricing tied to number of users, deal volume, integrations, and data retention/security posture.

## Real Moat

The moat is not the pipeline architecture. The moat is the accumulation of structured fund-specific judgment.

### 1. Proprietary Decision Ledger

Each fund creates labeled decision data:

- Invest, Pass, Monitor.
- Why the decision happened.
- What risks were acknowledged.
- What scores and confidence existed at the time.
- Whether the firm overrode the system.
- Which similar deals became precedent.

### 2. Switching Cost

Once Aethria holds years of:

- committee packets,
- decision rationales,
- risk acknowledgements,
- calibration changes,
- historical misses,
- partner overrides,
- and fund-specific thresholds,

leaving is painful because the firm loses its operating memory.

### 3. Workflow Embedding

The weekly partner meeting is habitual. If Aethria becomes the place where "what needs a ruling" is opened every Monday, it becomes operationally sticky.

### 4. Fund-Specific Calibration

The system improves not by generic model training, but by learning the fund's actual judgment pattern:

- which risks the firm tolerates,
- which sectors repeatedly stall,
- which scouts produce accepted deals,
- where thresholds are too strict or lenient,
- where human overrides happen.

### 5. Trust And Audit Trail

Venture firms are paranoid for good reason. Aethria's defensibility depends on being the trustworthy system:

- source lineage,
- prompt/policy version lineage,
- human ruling history,
- explicit missing-data behavior,
- exportable IC packs,
- clear data controls.

## What Is Not A Moat

- "We use AI."
- "We have agents."
- "We generate memos."
- "We have a Scout/Reviewer/Partner architecture."
- "We integrate with Supabase and Clerk."
- "Our UX feels institutional."

Those can support the product, but they are not defensibility.

## Proof Needed Before A Strong Seed Raise

Minimum credible proof:

- 3 named or clearly characterized design partners.
- 50-100 historical deals processed.
- 20+ live deals evaluated in shadow mode.
- Evidence of at least one saved decision, accelerated pass, or revived opportunity.
- Weekly usage by at least one GP or senior investor.
- Clear data-handling promise accepted by funds.

Best proof:

> "A partner used Aethria in Monday meeting to move three deals out of limbo, reject one that had been consuming attention for weeks, and revive one company because the system showed it matched a prior thesis pattern."
