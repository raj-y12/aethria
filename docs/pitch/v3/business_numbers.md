## Directional Business Model

Initial ICP:

- Emerging seed managers and solo/micro funds.
- Fund I/II, roughly $10M-$75M AUM.
- Lean teams evaluating hundreds of companies per year.

Pricing hypothesis:

- Beta paid pilot: $500-$1,500/month.
- Production emerging-manager plan: $6K-$18K/year.
- Institutional plan later: $25K-$75K/year with collaboration, integrations, retention, and security.

## Bottom-Up Example

Do not call this TAM. Call it first-market math.

Assumptions:

- 1,000 reachable emerging VC managers globally.
- 20% have enough deal volume and process pain to consider the product.
- 25% of those become serious prospects.
- 30% of serious prospects convert over time.
- Average annual contract value: $12K.

Math:

- 1,000 managers.
- 200 likely-fit funds.
- 50 serious prospects.
- 15 customers.
- 15 x $12K = $180K ARR from the first narrow wedge.

This is not venture-scale alone. It is the path to proof.

Expansion math:

- 150 customers at $12K = $1.8M ARR.
- 300 customers at $18K = $5.4M ARR.
- 100 institutional customers at $50K = $5M ARR.

The venture-scale case requires expanding from emerging-manager wedge into broader fund intelligence infrastructure.

## Usage Numbers That Matter More Than Market Numbers

Track these during pilots:

- Deals loaded.
- Deals opened in meeting.
- IC packs exported.
- Human rulings recorded.
- Adverse items acknowledged.
- Stale deals resolved.
- Partner overrides.
- Precedents reused.
- Time from first look to pass/monitor/invest.
- Weekly active GP usage.

## The Number To Put In The Deck Before Proof

Use a pilot milestone, not a fake market size:

> Next 60 days: 3 design partners, 60 historical deals, 10 live shadow deals, 1 fund using Aethria in a weekly partner meeting.

This tells the investor exactly what risk is being retired.
