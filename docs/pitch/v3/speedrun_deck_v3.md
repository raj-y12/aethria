## Slide 3 - Customer

**Title**

Start with emerging seed managers.

**Core message**

The first customer is not every VC. It is the lean emerging manager who wants institutional decision discipline before they can hire institutional process.

**Slide copy**

Initial ICP:

- Fund I/II.
- $10M-$75M AUM.
- Seed/pre-seed focus.
- Lean team, often solo GP or 2-4 person partnership.
- High inbound relative to team size.
- No dedicated internal systems team.

They do not need another CRM.

They need a way to prove, to themselves and LPs, that their judgment is disciplined.

**Visual**

Narrow wedge diagram: Emerging seed managers first, then micro-funds, then larger venture teams.

**Talk track**

"The mistake is saying 'VCs' as one customer. The first customer is a lean fund where one GP can say yes."

## Slide 4 - The Wedge

**Title**

20 historical deals before live pipeline trust.

**Core message**

We earn trust by starting with past decisions, not by asking for the firm's live proprietary pipeline.

**Slide copy**

Pilot:

- 5 invested deals.
- 5 correct passes.
- 5 regretted passes or misses.
- 5 ambiguous monitor deals.

Aethria outputs:

- decision ledger;
- risk postmortem;
- scoring consistency report;
- IC packs for example deals;
- where the fund was too strict, too loose, or too slow.

**Visual**

Input/output pilot graphic.

**Talk track**

"If I cannot get one fund to run 20 historical deals for free, I do not deserve to ask investors to fund GTM."

## Slide 5 - Why Existing Tools Do Not Own This

**Title**

The CRM owns relationships. Aethria owns rulings.

**Core message**

Existing tools record that a deal exists. Aethria records how the fund decided.

**Slide copy**

The judgment record:

- why the company fit the mandate;
- what risk blocked conviction;
- what evidence was missing;
- who owned the next action;
- what ruling was recorded;
- which precedent it resembles.

**Visual**

Two artifacts side by side:

- CRM row: company, stage, owner, last touch.

**Talk track**

"Aethria should integrate with CRMs later. It should not pretend to replace them now."

## Slide 6 - Habit

**Title**

The product wins only if it enters the meeting.

**Core message**

Switching cost starts after habit. The first habit is opening Aethria to decide what needs a ruling this week.

**Slide copy**

Weekly usage loop:

1. Monday queue opens.
2. Stale/adverse/escalated deals rise first.
3. GP assigns owner or records ruling.
4. IC pack is exported for serious deals.
5. Decision history updates.
6. Next Monday starts from memory, not rediscovery.

**Visual**

Weekly loop anchored to Monday meeting.

**Talk track**

"The moat is not memory in a database. The moat starts when the team uses that memory in the room."

## Slide 7 - Moat

**Title**

The fund's decision ledger compounds.

**Core message**

The workflow is copyable. A living decision ledger inside the firm's weekly cadence is not.

**Slide copy**

Every ruling stores:

- decision and rationale;
- score and confidence snapshot;
- risks acknowledged;
- policy version;
- partner override;
- precedent links;
- calibration signal.

If Aethria becomes the weekly decision surface, leaving means losing the operating memory of the fund.

**Visual**

Decision ledger growing over time, with precedent and calibration loops.

**Talk track**

"The moat is conditional: first habit, then memory, then switching cost."
