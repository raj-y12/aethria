## Fact 1: Real Fund Scene

The strongest opening requires one firsthand or directly sourced scene.

Acceptable sources:

- Raj personally interviewed the GP.
- The GP/design partner gave permission to anonymize the story.
- Raj observed the workflow directly.
- The scene comes from a live Aethria pilot.

Not acceptable:

- Podcast anecdote.
- Article quote.
- "A founder friend said this happens."
- Generic reconstruction from VC Twitter.

Minimum details needed:

- Fund type.
- Team size.
- Deal source.
- Company stage/sector.
- Where the decision lived today.
- What got stuck.
- What risk or missing evidence mattered.
- What happened next.
- Whether the GP agrees this is the real failure mode.

## Fact 2: Real Founder Workflow Story

The founder slide needs one true example of Raj structuring a messy workflow.

Minimum details needed:

- Organization/context.
- Raj's role.
- Workflow/problem.
- Users/stakeholders.
- What was messy before.
- What Raj built, documented, automated, analyzed, or changed.
- What artifact proves it.
- What Raj learned about expert users or operating controls.

If no strong EY example exists, use Aethria itself:

> I built Aethria because I kept seeing that the hard part was not generating more startup analysis; it was forcing every deal into a clear state: owner, risk, ruling, memory. The product is my proof that I can turn a vague workflow into an operating system. The gap is that I now need GPs inside live workflows to harden it.

This is less strong than an EY story, but it is honest.

## Application Rule

If neither a real fund scene nor real founder workflow story is available:

- Do not lead with a fake scene.
- Do not mention EY as an implied moat.
- Lead with product and narrow ICP.
- Say the next milestone is customer proof.
