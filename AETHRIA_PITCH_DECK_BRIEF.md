# Aethria Pitch Deck Brief

## 1. Executive Summary

Aethria is a venture decision operating system for solo GPs and emerging seed funds. It helps lean investment teams turn messy dealflow into structured weekly decisions: every company gets a state, risk register, missing-evidence list, next action, ruling, precedent, and fund memory.

This deck is for an accelerator-ready pitch, especially a16z Speedrun. The goal is not to present Aethria as a generic AI product, CRM, sourcing tool, or memo generator. The goal is to show that Aethria has a sharp workflow insight: solo and two-person seed funds do not need another place to store deals; they need a system that forces decisions.

The audience should come away feeling:

- the ICP is narrow and credible;
- the wedge is low-friction;
- the product already exists;
- the founder understands the next risk is weekly usage with real funds;
- Aethria feels like serious decision infrastructure, not productivity software.

Desired impression:

> This feels inevitable. This feels like serious infrastructure. This founder has a real wedge.

## 2. Brand And Design System

### Design Personality

The deck should feel cinematic, editorial, institutional, and restrained. It should look more like a decision terminal or high-end research system than a SaaS dashboard.

Use sparse, high-conviction layouts with large typography, intentional whitespace, thin dividers, and minimal interface fragments. The deck should feel premium and systematic, not playful or salesy.

### Color Palette

Primary dark blue:

`#001B47`

Primary warm off-white:

`#F4F1EA`

Use these as the dominant colors. The deck can alternate between dark-blue slides with warm off-white type and warm off-white slides with dark-blue type.

Avoid bright accent palettes. If an alert/risk color is needed, use it sparingly and in a muted institutional way, not as a neon SaaS accent.

### Typography

Use:

- **Libre Baskerville** only for the Aethria wordmark and rare hero moments.
- **Geist** for headings, body copy, and main slide language.
- **JetBrains Mono / JB Mono** for metadata, labels, timestamps, states, scores, tags, queue/risk elements, and system UI details.

Typography should be large and confident. Do not overfill slides with body copy. Slides should read quickly.

### Layout Principles

- One idea per slide.
- Large headline, sparse body.
- Use asymmetric compositions.
- Use editorial whitespace.
- Use thin rules/dividers.
- Use low-density product UI fragments.
- Let product states and decision artifacts carry the story.
- Avoid grid-heavy dashboards unless intentionally cropped into a cinematic interface moment.

### Texture And Surface

Subtle grain/noise texture is encouraged. It should be barely visible and should make the deck feel tactile and premium.

Soft blur panels may be used for product-inspired UI layers, but avoid glossy SaaS blur effects. The product should feel serious, not decorative.

### UI Rules

Product-inspired UI should feel like a decision terminal:

- queue states;
- risk register;
- missing evidence;
- recommendation state;
- ruling controls;
- precedent links;
- timestamps;
- evidence lineage;
- IC export status.

UI should be minimal, monochrome, and legible. Avoid generic CRM-looking pipeline cards.

### Image Rules

Do not use stock photography, cartoon startup illustrations, robot imagery, fake AI graphics, or generic 3D SaaS shapes.

If real product screenshots are not visually strong enough, recreate them as stylized product mockups using real product language and layout logic.

### Avoid

- generic AI graphics;
- robot imagery;
- neon SaaS glow;
- bright gradients;
- cartoon startup illustrations;
- overfilled slides;
- CRM-style dashboards;
- productivity app visuals;
- “AI agent” clichés;
- “revolutionizing VC” language.

## 3. Product Positioning

### What Aethria Is

Aethria is venture decision infrastructure for solo and emerging seed managers. It is a decision command center that turns dealflow into weekly rulings.

It tracks:

- company state;
- thesis alignment;
- structured score;
- adverse signals;
- missing evidence;
- next action;
- ruling;
- rationale;
- precedent;
- fund memory.

Core positioning:

> Solo GPs do not need another place to store deals. They need a way to force decisions.

### What Aethria Is Not

Aethria is not:

- an AI copilot;
- a CRM;
- a sourcing tool;
- a memo generator;
- a generic workflow tool;
- a replacement for investor judgment.

The product supports judgment. It does not pretend to replace it.

### Category Language

Use:

- venture decision infrastructure;
- weekly decision reset;
- fund operating memory;
- conviction and ruling system for emerging managers;
- decision command center.

Avoid:

- AI for VC;
- autonomous investment analyst;
- venture copilot;
- memo automation;
- dealflow CRM.

## 4. Target Customer

Initial ICP:

- solo GP or two-person seed fund;
- Fund I or Fund II;
- `$10M-$25M` AUM;
- seed/pre-seed focus;
- no full-time analyst;
- no formal investment committee;
- currently using spreadsheets, Notion, Airtable, email, WhatsApp, lightweight CRM, and memory.

This is the right starting customer because Aethria can become the first institutional process rather than trying to displace an established one.

For a larger fund, Aethria would need to integrate into existing CRM, analyst, and IC workflows. For the initial ICP, Aethria can create the weekly decision cadence from scratch.

Customer pain:

- too many “interesting” companies stay unresolved;
- pass decisions disappear;
- missing evidence is tracked informally;
- risks live in memory or chat;
- no one can consistently revisit why a company was passed, monitored, or advanced;
- the fund wants institutional discipline before it has institutional headcount.

## 5. Narrative Arc

The deck should move quickly:

1. **Product truth:** venture software remembers companies; Aethria remembers decisions.
2. **Product:** Aethria creates a weekly decision reset.
3. **Customer:** the wedge is solo and two-person emerging seed funds.
4. **Wedge:** start with historical decisions, not live pipeline trust.
5. **Workflow:** queue, risk, action, ruling, memory.
6. **Category:** CRM manages relationships; Aethria manages conviction.
7. **Habit and moat:** fund memory compounds only if Aethria becomes the weekly decision surface.
8. **Proof plan:** product exists; next risk is weekly usage.
9. **Founder:** technical founder, product built, workflow must now be hardened with real funds.
10. **Ask:** use Speedrun to convert a built system into repeatable fund workflow.
11. **Close:** venture has systems for sourcing; Aethria is building the system for decisions.

The narrative should be direct, not philosophical. The deck should not over-claim traction. It should show a precise wedge and a credible next milestone.

## 6. Slide-By-Slide Brief

### Slide 1 — Product Truth

**Slide objective**

Establish the core insight immediately: Aethria is about decisions, not deal storage.

**Title**

Venture software remembers companies.  
Aethria remembers decisions.

**Core message**

Solo GPs do not need another place to store deals. They need a system that forces decisions.

**Exact slide copy**

Early-stage investing is operationally fragmented:

- notes live across spreadsheets, Notion, email, and memory;
- “interesting” companies remain unresolved for weeks;
- risks are tracked informally;
- pass decisions disappear;
- conviction is difficult to revisit consistently.

Aethria turns pipeline chaos into a weekly decision process.

**Visual direction**

Before/after composition:

- left side: scattered notes, browser tabs, inbox fragments, WhatsApp-style message blocks, spreadsheet rows;
- right side: Aethria queue with state, risk, next action, ruling, precedent.

Keep it abstracted and premium. Do not make it look like a literal messy desktop screenshot.

**Notes for designer**

Use a strong split layout or asymmetric before/after. Make the Aethria side calmer, darker, and more structured. Use JetBrains Mono for states like `READY`, `STALE`, `RISK`, `PASS`, `MONITOR`, `ADVANCE`.

**What not to do**

Do not use stock photos, cartoons, or a generic CRM kanban board.

### Slide 2 — Product

**Slide objective**

Show the actual product behavior: a weekly decision reset.

**Title**

The weekly decision reset.

**Core message**

Every company must move toward a ruling.

**Exact slide copy**

Every week, the GP opens Aethria and sees:

- ready decisions;
- stale opportunities;
- unresolved diligence;
- adverse signals;
- missing evidence;
- recommendation state;
- pass / monitor / advance actions;
- IC-ready exports when needed.

Aethria exists so no deal stays in limbo indefinitely.

**Visual direction**

Real or product-inspired queue + memo + risk register. Use a cinematic product crop. The interface should show a queue on one side and a selected company decision surface on the other.

**Notes for designer**

Make this the first strong product slide. It should feel real. Use low-density UI and large whitespace around the product frame.

**What not to do**

Do not show a fake colorful dashboard. Do not make the product look like a CRM pipeline.

### Slide 3 — Customer

**Slide objective**

Make the ICP obvious and credible.

**Title**

Built for the first institutional process.

**Core message**

Start where process does not already exist.

**Exact slide copy**

Initial ICP:

- solo GP or two-person seed fund;
- Fund I or Fund II;
- `$10M-$25M` AUM;
- no full-time analyst;
- no formal investment committee;
- lightweight CRM + fragmented communication workflow.

These funds need institutional discipline before institutional headcount.

**Visual direction**

One-person fund operating model. Show a simple operating map: GP at center, surrounded by inbound, intros, notes, follow-ups, diligence, LP expectations, and decisions.

**Notes for designer**

The visual should feel sober and precise. This slide is about specificity, not market size.

**What not to do**

Do not imply the customer is all VCs. Do not show a large enterprise org chart.

### Slide 4 — Wedge

**Slide objective**

Explain the low-friction pilot motion and why trust starts with historical decisions.

**Title**

Start with historical decisions.

**Core message**

Trust is earned on old decisions before live workflow.

**Exact slide copy**

Pilot structure:

- 5 investments;
- 5 correct passes;
- 5 missed opportunities;
- 5 unresolved or ambiguous cases.

Aethria returns:

- decision ledger;
- risk postmortems;
- scoring consistency analysis;
- precedent mapping;
- examples of delayed or weak conviction.

The first product interaction is not sourcing.  
It is self-audit.

**Visual direction**

20-deal input flowing into structured decision system. Show 20 small deal tiles becoming a decision ledger, risk map, and precedent map.

**Notes for designer**

This slide should make the wedge feel low-risk and practical. The phrase “self-audit” can be visually emphasized.

**What not to do**

Do not show autonomous sourcing or live pipeline ingestion as the first step.

### Slide 5 — Workflow

**Slide objective**

Show the operating loop and reinforce that decisions are the product center.

**Title**

Queue → risk → action → ruling → memory

**Core message**

The product is organized around decisions, not notes.

**Exact slide copy**

Each company receives:

- thesis alignment;
- structured scoring;
- adverse register;
- missing evidence;
- next action;

Aethria separates weak companies from unresolved diligence.

The loop should feel operational, not conceptual. Each stage should have one small UI artifact.

**What not to do**

Do not make this a generic AI agent pipeline diagram.

Position Aethria against existing tools without overclaiming category dominance.

The CRM tracks who the GP knows. Aethria tracks what the fund decided and why.

Traditional CRM:

Aethria:

Over time, the fund develops reusable institutional judgment.

Weekly reset → decision ledger → memory graph. Show repeated weekly resets accumulating into a structured fund memory layer.

**Notes for designer**

This slide should feel serious and cumulative. Use small repeated marks or rows to show compounding memory.

**What not to do**

Do not imply the moat exists before usage. Do not use network-effect language.

Venture has systems for sourcing.  
Aethria is building the system for decisions.

Minimal closing slide. Use the Aethria wordmark in Libre Baskerville. Large type. No extra clutter.

**Notes for designer**

This should feel like a confident final statement. It should be quiet, not loud.

**What not to do**

Do not add bullets, diagrams, or extra explanation.

Screenshots should be cleaned, cropped, and art-directed. Raw UI can be used only if it looks polished and legible.

If raw screenshots are visually noisy, recreate them as stylized product mockups. The mockups should still use real product concepts:

- decision queue;

- adverse register;

- pass / monitor / advance;
- precedent linkage;
- IC export.

Avoid showing too much UI at once. Use cinematic crops and layered panels.

Product visuals should prove the product exists while still matching the deck aesthetic.

## 9. Designer Instructions

### Pacing

The deck should move quickly. Product appears by slide 2. The ICP is clear by slide 3. The wedge is clear by slide 4.

### Hierarchy

Each slide should communicate one idea:

- one headline;
- one core visual;
- one short copy block;
- no dense paragraphs unless absolutely necessary.

### Sparsity

Keep slides sparse. Do not try to fit the entire brief into the deck. The deck should feel high-conviction, not exhaustive.

### Product Reality

The product should feel real. Use actual language from the product where possible:

- ready decisions;
- stale opportunities;
- adverse register;
- missing evidence;
- final ruling;
- conviction state;
- IC export;
- precedent.

### Visual Rhythm

Alternate between:

- statement slides;
- product artifact slides;
- workflow slides;
- proof/milestone slides.

The deck should not feel like 11 identical text slides.

## 10. Final Quality Bar

Before delivery, check:

- Does every slide communicate one idea?
- Does the deck avoid AI SaaS clichés?
- Does it avoid “revolutionizing VC” language?
- Does the deck feel institutional?
- Does the product feel real?
- Is the ICP obvious?
- Is the wedge obvious?
- Is the 60-day plan clear?
- Does the deck show why Aethria is not a CRM?
- Does the habit/moat logic feel honest?
- Does the founder slide avoid fake authority?
- Does the deck end with conviction?

The final deck should feel like serious investment decision infrastructure for a very specific customer, not generic software for venture capital.
